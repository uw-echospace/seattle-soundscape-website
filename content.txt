# --- Program Description
[how.heading] AudioMoth Student Research Program
[how.subhead] No experience needed!
[how.imageAlt] A small brown bat in flight
[how.imageCredit] Durham, Michael. “Little Brown Myotis (Myotis Lucifugus)” Bats Northwest. https://www.batsnorthwest.org/meet-washingtons-bats

[how.step.1.title] Pick up your AudioMoth kit
[how.step.1.body] Come to the onboarding session to learn about the Greater Seattle Soundscape Monitoring Project, how to setup and deploy [AudioMoth recorder units](https://www.openacousticdevices.info/audiomoth), and analysis tools we use!

[how.step.2.title] Record and collect data
[how.step.2.body] Send your AudioMoth out into the field, periodically retrieving data and replacing batteries during the recording period. You'll run an analysis using machine learning models to detect local species and generate insights about your location's soundscape.

[how.step.3.title] View your findings and the impact
[how.step.3.body] Upload data to the cloud, comparing your findings to others. See the generated fingerprint of detected species over the recording period, contributing to the long-term understanding of our ecosystem.

# --- Impact
[mission.quote] The impact *your data* has on our environment.
[mission.body.1.text] Passive acoustic monitoring, or PAM, uses continuously deployed recorders to collect audio data at a particular location. By enlisting the help of community scientists (like you!) to collect this data, the impact and breadth of projects like this one greatly increases.
[mission.body.2.text] The collected recordings are crucial to maintaining an ongoing dataset used by scientists to track local population trends over time. Through extensive data collection, we learn more about how climate change, human development, and many other factors can affect biodiversity in our local soundscape.


# --- FAQ
[faq.heading] Frequently Asked Questions

[faq.1.q] Do I need to buy or bring any equipment?
[faq.1.a] The AudioMoth kits, which include the recorder, case, batteries, and SD cards, are provided by Echospace and funded by the Student Technology Fee. You'll only need a laptop to run the analysis software.

[faq.2.q] Do I need any background in bioacoustics, ecology, or computer science?
[faq.2.a] No! We'll show you how to setup the AudioMoth, understand how it works, and provide an out-of-the-box app to analyze the data. However, if you have experience, you'll be able to dig deeper into the data as you wish!

[faq.3.q] Where can I put my AudioMoth?
[faq.3.a] You can adopt any location in the Greater Seattle or King County area (that includes Bellevue, Everett, Bainbridge Island, Federal Way, or anywhere nearby)! We can help you find a location or you can pick one that's convenient for you. Throughout your recording period, you'll be responsible for coming back to check-in on the location.

[faq.4.q] Who runs this program?
[faq.4.a] The Greater Seattle Soundscape Monitoring Project is run by the Echospace Group. We study acoustic sensing in terrestrial and aquatic animals, as well as run projects using passive acoustic monitoring in the Union Bay Natural Area. You can learn more about [our projects here](https://uw-echospace.github.io/#projects).

[faq.5.q] How can I get help with my equipment or software if I need it?
[faq.5.a] We host office hours once a month! We'll be able to assist with regular SD card swaps, technical issues, new equipment pickup, and answering any questions you may have. You can also come to learn more about the project, as we share some additional information exciting each month--everyone is invited! Generally, office hours are on weekday evenings, but we also offer 1-on-1 meetings if you can't make them or need support sooner. 

[faq.6.q] How do I upload my data?
[faq.6.a] For all management-related tasks, log into our [webportal](/login/)! This is where you can upload data, request new SD cards, make appointments, stay up-to-date, and more. While some analysis data will be uploaded through the webportal, the large file sizes of recordings make it necessary that we do also request SD cards be swapped in-person.

[faq.7.q] Who is this program available to?
[faq.7.a] Currently, the Seattle Soundscape Monitoring Program is only available for University of Washington affiliates, including undergraduate and graduate students, faculty, and staff members. If you have an education-related request for our AudioMoths or you aren't at the University of Washington, please contact us at [seattle.soundscape.uw@gmail.com](mailto:seattle.soundscape.uw@gmail.com)!

[faq.8.q] Am I committed to recording for the entire quarter?
[faq.8.a] If you'd prefer to not record during a busy time (for example, during midterms), you're free to skip a few weeks of recordings. If you know you won't be able to continue recording or you're not interested in the program anymore, feel free to drop off your equipment at office hours.

[faq.9.q] Am I guaranteed an AudioMoth?
[faq.9.a] We will try our best to make sure every participant has access to a recording kit. If demand exceeds capacity, we'll make sure to prioritize participants on the waitlist for future quarters.
